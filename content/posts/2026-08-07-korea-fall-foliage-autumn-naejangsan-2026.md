Korea fall foliage season is, without exaggeration, one of the best reasons to visit this country — and after spending several autumns chasing colour across the peninsula, I can tell you exactly where to go, when to show up, and what rookie mistakes to avoid. Whether you're planning a single weekend escape from Seoul or a multi-city foliage road trip, this guide has you covered.

---

## Why Autumn in Korea Is Worth Planning Your Whole Trip Around

Korea's fall foliage season is a genuine spectacle — the combination of deciduous maples, ginkgos, and Korean hornbeam trees turns mountainsides into an almost unreal palette of crimson, gold, and orange. According to [Korea Tourism Organization (VisitKorea)](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr), the autumn foliage season is consistently one of the top three reasons international visitors cite for choosing Korea as a destination (as of August 2026).

What makes Korea's autumn different from, say, Japan's or New England's? The density. Mountains here are steep and heavily forested, which means the colour saturation is intense and the photo opportunities are almost overwhelming. Add in the fact that many of the best viewing spots are tied to Buddhist temples — ancient stone pagodas framed by fiery maples — and you've got a combination that's hard to beat anywhere in the world.

The foliage typically kicks off in the northern mountains and higher elevations in early October, then cascades southward, reaching the warmer regions of Jeolla-do and coastal areas by mid-to-late November. **Peak colour nationwide usually falls between October 20 and November 10**, though climate patterns in 2026 should be confirmed closer to the date at the [Korea Meteorological Administration](https://www.weather.go.kr/w/index.do).

---

## Naejangsan National Park: Korea's Undisputed Foliage Capital

Naejangsan is the one place every serious autumn traveller in Korea needs to experience at least once. Located in North Jeolla Province (Jeollabuk-do), about **240 km south of Seoul**, Naejangsan National Park is famous for its tunnel of maple trees leading to Naejangsa Temple — a path so photogenic it genuinely looks photoshopped when the leaves hit peak red.

I first visited on a crisp Saturday in late October and was genuinely unprepared for how red "red" could actually be. The maple canopy over the main trail forms a near-complete arch, and when the morning light hits it at around 8–9 a.m., the whole forest glows.

**Practical details for Naejangsan (as of August 2026):**

| Detail | Info |
|---|---|
| Peak foliage window | Approx. late October – early November |
| Entry fee | Adults ₩3,000 / Children ₩1,000 (verify at [Korea National Park Service](https://www.knps.or.kr/)) |
| Best time to arrive | Weekdays before 9 a.m. (weekends get crowded by 10 a.m.) |
| From Seoul | KTX to Jeongeup Station (~1 hr 40 min), then bus or taxi (~20 min) |
| From Gwangju | Direct bus ~1 hr |

The cable car (₩10,000 round trip, subject to change) whisks you up for aerial views over the valley — absolutely worth it on a clear day. I'd recommend doing the cable car first thing, then hiking down through the maple tunnel on foot. Give yourself a full day.

> 💡 **Insider tip:** Book your KTX tickets at least 2–3 weeks in advance through [Korail](https://www.letskorail.com/). Naejangsan weekend trains sell out fast in October.

## Beyond Naejangsan: Five More Spots That Deserve Your Time

Naejangsan gets the headlines, but Korea's autumn goes far deeper than one national park. Here are five spots that consistently deliver stunning colour — and often with far smaller crowds.

**1. Seoraksan National Park (Gangwon Province)**
Seoraksan usually sees the *first* significant colour change on the mainland, typically in early-to-mid October. The jagged granite peaks dusted in red and orange are iconic. Take the cable car up Gwongeumseong for a panorama that rivals anything in the country.

**2. Odaesan National Park (Gangwon Province)**
Quieter than Seoraksan but just as beautiful, Odaesan's beech and maple forests around Woljeongsa Temple turn a softer, more golden hue. Great for people who want contemplative autumn walks rather than weekend-crowd chaos.

**3. Jirisan National Park (South/North Gyeongsang & Jeolla Provinces)**
Korea's largest national park by area rewards those willing to hike. The Baemsagol Valley in particular — a narrow ravine where the trail follows a stream through a maple canopy — is one of the most underrated autumn scenes in the country.

**4. Bukhansan National Park (Seoul)**
Yes, you can see outstanding foliage without leaving Seoul. Bukhansan turns vivid red and gold from late October, and the sight of autumn colour against the city skyline is uniquely Korean. Take Line 3 to Gupabal or Line 4 to Suyu station.

**5. Damyang Metasequoia Road (South Jeolla Province)**
Not a mountain, but worth the detour. A 8.5 km avenue of towering metasequoia trees turns a warm amber in November. Often paired with a Naejangsan trip since Damyang is only about 40 minutes away by car.

## How to Time Your Visit: A Foliage Calendar

Getting the timing right is everything — show up a week too early and it's still green; a week too late and the trees are bare. Here's how to read the season.

| Region / Spot | Typical Peak Window |
|---|---|
| Seoraksan (Gangwon) | Oct 5 – Oct 20 |
| Odaesan (Gangwon) | Oct 10 – Oct 25 |
| Bukhansan (Seoul) | Oct 20 – Nov 5 |
| Naejangsan (Jeolla) | Oct 25 – Nov 10 |
| Jirisan (South) | Oct 28 – Nov 12 |
| Damyang (Jeolla) | Nov 1 – Nov 15 |

These windows are based on historical averages — actual peak dates shift year to year depending on summer temperatures and first-frost timing. **The Korea Meteorological Administration typically releases its official foliage forecast in late September**, so I strongly recommend checking [weather.go.kr](https://www.weather.go.kr/w/index.do) once that forecast drops.

A few rules I swear by:
1. **Mid-week visits are non-negotiable for popular spots.** Naejangsan on a Sunday in late October is wall-to-wall people; the same trail on a Tuesday morning is peaceful.
2. **Book accommodation 6–8 weeks out.** Guesthouses near Naejangsan and Seoraksan fill up completely during peak weekends.
3. **Layer up.** Mornings in the mountains can be near-freezing in November; afternoons warm to 15–18°C.

## Getting Around Korea for the Foliage Season: Transport Tips

Korea's rail and bus network makes a multi-spot foliage trip surprisingly straightforward, even without a car.

**Train (KTX / ITX):** The backbone of any foliage itinerary. Seoul to Jeongeup (for Naejangsan) takes roughly **1 hour 40 minutes on KTX**. Seoul to Dong-Incheon or Gangneung (for Seoraksan access) is covered by KTX-Eum. Book at [Korail](https://www.letskorail.com/) or through the Korail Talk app.

**Intercity Bus:** For spots not directly on the rail network (Damyang, some Jirisan trailheads), the express bus system is fast and affordable. Seoul's Central City Terminal and East Seoul Terminal cover most destinations.

**Rental Car:** If you want maximum flexibility — especially for Jirisan's multiple valley entrances — renting a car makes a lot of sense. Rates from major Korean rental companies (Lotte Rent-a-Car, SK Rent-a-Car) run roughly ₩60,000–₩100,000/day for a compact (as of August 2026; confirm current rates directly).

**Organised Tours:** KTO and various Seoul-based operators run dedicated autumn foliage day tours to Naejangsan in October–November, which can be a stress-free option if you prefer not to navigate independently. Check the [VisitKorea official site](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr) for curated tour listings.

## Autumn in Korea Practical Checklist

Before you book that flight, run through this list:

- [ ] **Check the KMA foliage forecast** (released late September) at [weather.go.kr](https://www.weather.go.kr/w/index.do)
- [ ] **Book KTX tickets** at least 2–3 weeks in advance via [Korail](https://www.letskorail.com/)
- [ ] **Reserve accommodation** 6–8 weeks ahead for Naejangsan / Seoraksan areas
- [ ] **Pack layers:** fleece, a light waterproof shell, and comfortable hiking shoes
- [ ] **Plan for weekday visits** at popular spots to avoid weekend crowds
- [ ] **Confirm entry fees and park hours** at [Korea National Park Service](https://www.knps.or.kr/) before you go — fees and hours are subject to change
- [ ] **Download the KakaoMap or Naver Map app** for offline navigation in mountain areas
- [ ] **Carry cash** — smaller temple shops and mountain food stalls often don't accept cards

Autumn in Korea is one of those experiences that genuinely lives up to the hype — and then some. Plan smart, time it right, and you'll come home with photos and memories that will make your friends seriously reconsider their holiday plans.
