If you've been dreaming about **Busan beaches**, planning a summer trip to Korea, and staring at two names — Haeundae and Gwangalli — wondering which one is actually worth your time (or whether you can fit in both), this guide is exactly what you need. I've spent multiple summers in Busan, eaten my weight in dwaeji-gukbap, and walked both shores at golden hour and midnight. Here's the honest breakdown, the food you shouldn't miss, and a few things the algorithm never tells you.

**The short answer:** Go to Haeundae if you want the full Korean summer beach experience — crowds, carnival energy, and convenience. Go to Gwangalli if you want a more local, café-lined, photo-worthy evening with the bridge as your backdrop. Ideally, do both — they're only about 20 minutes apart by subway.

---

## Haeundae Beach: Korea's Most Famous Shoreline Is Worth the Hype

Haeundae is the undisputed headline act of Busan's coast — **1.5 km of white sand** packed with beach umbrella rentals, food stalls, and a skyline of luxury hotels that rises behind the shore like a film set. As of August 2026, it remains the single most visited beach in Korea, drawing over **1.5 million visitors during peak summer weeks** alone according to [VisitKorea](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr).

When I first walked onto Haeundae on a Saturday in late July, I genuinely couldn't see the sand — that's not an exaggeration, that's just what peak season looks like. But here's what surprised me: it doesn't feel chaotic. It feels *alive*. Families are grilling corn on portable stoves, groups of friends have claimed their square metre of sand like it's territory, and the water is surprisingly clean and well-patrolled by lifeguards.

**Practical basics for Haeundae:**

| Detail | Info (as of August 2026) |
|---|---|
| Beach length | ~1.5 km |
| Nearest subway | Haeundae Station (Line 2), Exit 3 or 5 |
| Umbrella + sunbed rental | approx. ₩15,000–₩20,000/day per set |
| Lifeguard season | June–August (swimming zone roped off) |
| Best time to visit | Weekday mornings before 10 a.m. |

The surrounding area — especially **Haeundae Market** and the Mipo neighbourhood at the east end of the beach — is where the real culinary action happens. Don't skip it.

---

## Gwangalli Beach: The Moodier, More Photogenic Rival

Gwangalli is the beach Busan locals actually go to when they want to enjoy themselves rather than perform "being at the beach." It's shorter (**about 900 m**), the sand is slightly coarser, and the swimming is more limited — but none of that matters when you're sitting at a beachfront café at 9 p.m. watching the **Gwangan Bridge** (officially Gwangandaegyo) light up in neon across the water.

I'll be honest: during the day, Gwangalli is pleasant but not dramatically different from Haeundae. The magic happens at dusk. The bridge — **7.4 km long, one of Korea's longest** — transforms the entire bay into a cinematic backdrop. Every café and restaurant facing the water fills up fast, and the street behind the beach (known colloquially as "the Gwangalli strip") buzzes with indie bars, craft beer spots, and pojangmacha (street food tents).

**Practical basics for Gwangalli:**

| Detail | Info (as of August 2026) |
|---|---|
| Beach length | ~900 m |
| Nearest subway | Gwangalli Station (Line 2), Exit 3, or Geumnyeonsan Station (Line 2), Exit 7 |
| Umbrella + sunbed rental | approx. ₩12,000–₩18,000/day per set |
| Gwangan Bridge light show | Nightly (check [Busan City official site](https://www.busan.go.kr/eng) for seasonal schedules) |
| Best time to visit | Evenings, especially weekdays |

One more thing: every August, Busan hosts the **Busan Sea Festival** centred around both beaches — Gwangalli in particular hosts drone and fireworks shows that are genuinely breathtaking. As of August 2026 the festival is ongoing; check the [Busan Tourism Organization](https://bto.or.kr/eng) for exact show dates and times since schedules change annually.

## Haeundae vs Gwangalli: A Side-by-Side Comparison

Both beaches are worth visiting, but they cater to different moods and itineraries. Here's how they stack up on the things that actually matter to travellers:

| Category | Haeundae | Gwangalli |
|---|---|---|
| Vibe | High-energy, resort town | Relaxed, café-bar culture |
| Crowd level (peak summer) | Extremely busy | Busy but manageable |
| Nightlife | Clubs near The Bay 101 area | Indie bars, beachfront cafés |
| Family-friendly | ✅ Strong (playgrounds, patrol) | ✅ Good (calmer waters) |
| Instagrammability | Sunrise over water | Gwangan Bridge at night |
| Food scene | Markets, seafood, bunsik | Cafés, fusion, street tents |
| Proximity to other sights | Dongbaek Island, BIFF Square nearby | Millak Neighbourhood Market |
| Budget | Slightly pricier area | Slightly more wallet-friendly |

**My honest verdict:** If you only have one day in Busan, go to Haeundae in the morning, then subway over to Gwangalli for dinner and the night view. You'll cover both worlds without rushing.

## What to Eat Near Haeundae Beach

The food near Haeundae is some of the best you'll find anywhere in Korea — and I'm not saying that lightly. **Haeundae Market** (해운대시장), a short walk from the beach, is where you should go first. Here's what to eat:

1. **Ssiat Hotteok (씨앗호떡)** — A Busan-specific spin on the classic street pancake, stuffed with seeds, honey, and glass noodles. The original stall inside Haeundae Market (look for the longest queue) has been operating for decades. Expect to pay around **₩1,500–₩2,000 per piece** (as of August 2026).
2. **Mulhoe (물회)** — Cold raw fish soup with a spicy, vinegary gochujang broth poured over ice. Perfect after a sweaty morning on the sand. Many seafood restaurants on the market's outer edge serve it.
3. **Haeundae Amso Galbi-jip area** — If you want grilled beef short ribs (galbi), the cluster of restaurants on the road behind the Grand Hotel is legendary locally. Prices vary; budget around **₩40,000–₩60,000 for two people** with sides.
4. **Dongbaek Island cafés** — Walk the 15-minute loop around Dongbaek Island (Nurimaru APEC House is there) and stop at one of the cliffside cafés for an Americano with a sea view.

## What to Eat Near Gwangalli Beach

Gwangalli's food scene skews younger and more eclectic — think craft beer and tacos next to a haemul pajeon (seafood pancake) stall.

1. **Millak Rawfish Town (민락횟집타운)** — This covered market just east of Gwangalli beach is a local institution. You buy fresh raw fish (hoe) from ground-floor fishmongers, then carry it upstairs to a restaurant that provides the banchan, ganjang (soy dipping sauce), and soju. Budget roughly **₩30,000–₩50,000 per person** depending on what fish you choose (as of August 2026).
2. **Gwangalli Eopseora Market (없어라 시장)** — A night market running on weekends in summer with local vendors selling everything from tteokbokki to artisan desserts. Lively and very photogenic.
3. **Beachfront café crawl** — The strip facing the bridge is lined with specialty coffee shops, many of which serve brunch into the afternoon. I'd particularly recommend arriving around 5–6 p.m., ordering something cold, and watching the bridge transition from sunset orange to full neon blue.
4. **Makgeolli pojangmacha** — Look for the blue tarpaulin tents along the beach's north end on weekend evenings. Traditional rice wine (makgeolli) with haemul pajeon here costs around **₩15,000–₩20,000** and is non-negotiable.

## Getting to Both Beaches: Transport Tips

Both beaches are extremely well-connected on **Busan Metro Line 2** (the green line), making it easy to do a beach-hop in a single day without taxis or stress.

1. From Busan Station (KTX arrival point): Take Line 1 to Seomyeon, transfer to Line 2, then continue to Gwangalli (5 stops) or Haeundae (8 stops).
2. Between Gwangalli and Haeundae: Just 3 stops on Line 2 — **about 7 minutes** on the train.
3. T-money card (available at any convenience store) works on metro, buses, and most taxis. Top it up as you go.
4. From Haeundae, Bus 1003 runs along the coast and is a scenic alternative to the subway — great if you want to see the Mipo and Songjeong neighbourhoods en route.

Single metro fare is approximately **₩1,500–₩1,900** depending on distance (as of August 2026). Always verify current fares and route updates via the [Busan Metro official site](https://www.humetro.busan.kr/eng/).

## Your Busan Beach Trip: Quick Checklist

Before you head out, run through this:

- [ ] Check the **Busan Sea Festival schedule** at [Busan Tourism Organization](https://bto.or.kr/eng) — fireworks and drone shows sell out viewing spots fast
- [ ] Arrive at Haeundae **before 10 a.m.** on weekdays if you want a sunbed without a 30-minute wait
- [ ] Load your **T-money card** on arrival — line transfers are easy and cheap
- [ ] Book any beachfront **café or restaurant seats** at Gwangalli a day ahead if visiting on a weekend evening
- [ ] Carry cash for market stalls (Haeundae Market, Millak) — many vendors don't take cards
- [ ] Reapply **SPF 50** — the Busan sun in August is serious
- [ ] Prices, festival schedules, and rental rates change seasonally — always **confirm details on official Busan tourism sites** before your trip

Busan's beaches are genuinely one of the best summer experiences in all of Asia. Pick your vibe, eat everything, and don't miss that bridge at night.
