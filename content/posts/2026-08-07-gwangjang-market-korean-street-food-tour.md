If you've ever wondered where to find the most authentic Korean street food in Seoul, the answer is Gwangjang Market — and after spending a full Saturday morning working my way through every alley of the place, I can tell you it lives up to every bit of its legendary reputation.

Gwangjang Market (광장시장) sits in the Jongno district of central Seoul and is widely regarded as one of Korea's oldest and most iconic traditional markets. Whether you're deep into K-culture, planning your first trip to Korea, or just a serious foodie hunting the real thing, this korean street food guide will walk you through exactly what to eat, what it costs (as of August 2026), and how to make the most of a visit without getting overwhelmed.

---

## Why Gwangjang Market Is the Heart of Korean Street Food Culture

Gwangjang Market is more than a market — it's a 120-year-old institution that has shaped how Seoul eats.

Established in **1905**, Gwangjang is widely cited as Korea's first permanent market, and the food alley at its center has been a beloved institution for generations. According to [VisitKorea](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/), the market draws over **100,000 visitors per day** on peak weekends (as of July 2026), and a significant portion of those crowds come purely for the food stalls lining the covered indoor alleys.

What makes it different from a modern food hall or a touristy night market is the genuine mix of customers. On any given morning you'll find elderly Seoul residents who've eaten here for decades, office workers grabbing a quick lunch, and yes, international travelers clutching their phones. Nobody minds — the vendors are used to all of it, and the chaos is part of the charm. When I visited on a summer Saturday, the air was thick with the smell of sesame oil and frying batter, and I honestly didn't want to leave.

## The Must-Eat Dishes: Your Gwangjang Market Food Checklist

Every stall at Gwangjang specializes, so knowing what to order before you walk in saves time and stomach space.

Here are the dishes you absolutely cannot leave without trying:

### 1. Bindaetteok (빈대떡) — Mung Bean Pancakes
This is **the** signature dish of Gwangjang Market. Bindaetteok are thick, crispy-edged pancakes made from ground mung beans, mixed with kimchi, pork, and green onions, then fried on a flat griddle right in front of you. The outside is golden and crackling; the inside is dense and savory. One pancake runs about **₩4,000–₩5,000** (as of August 2026). Order two. Trust me.

### 2. Yukhoe (육회) — Korean Raw Beef
Yukhoe is Korea's answer to beef tartare — thin strips of raw beef seasoned with sesame oil, garlic, pear, and a raw egg yolk on top. It sounds intimidating but it's silky, sweet-savory, and genuinely one of the best things I've eaten in Seoul. A serving costs around **₩10,000–₩15,000** depending on the stall. The vendors at Gwangjang are known for sourcing fresh beef daily, which is exactly why you should eat this here and not just anywhere.

### 3. Mayak Gimbap (마약 김밥) — "Narcotic" Rice Rolls
The nickname says it all. These are tiny, bite-sized gimbap rolls stuffed with pickled radish, spinach, and sesame-flecked rice, served with a mustard-soy dipping sauce. They're called "mayak" (narcotic) because you literally cannot stop eating them. A portion of about 10 rolls is around **₩3,000–₩4,000** — one of the best value bites in the entire market.

### 4. Seonjitguk (선지국) — Blood Soup
Not for the faint-hearted, but absolutely worth it if you want to eat like a true Seoul local. Seonjitguk is a deeply savory beef broth soup loaded with congealed ox blood, bean sprouts, and green onions. A bowl costs around **₩8,000–₩10,000** and pairs perfectly with a cold beer from a nearby stall.

### 5. Tteok (떡) — Korean Rice Cakes
The upper floor and surrounding stalls are stacked high with handmade rice cakes in every color and flavor — mugwort, sesame, red bean, chestnut. These are sold by weight or by box, and they make an excellent gift to take home. Prices vary widely but a small assorted box starts around **₩5,000**.

## Price Guide: What to Budget for a Gwangjang Market Food Tour

One of the best things about Gwangjang is how affordable it is — this is real, local Korean street food, not tourist-trap pricing.

| Dish | Approx. Price (Aug 2026) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Bindaetteok (mung bean pancake) | ₩4,000–₩5,000 each | Best eaten fresh off the griddle |
| Mayak Gimbap (10 pcs) | ₩3,000–₩4,000 | Dip in mustard sauce |
| Yukhoe (raw beef) | ₩10,000–₩15,000 | Order with extra egg yolk |
| Seonjitguk (blood soup) | ₩8,000–₩10,000 | Filling meal on its own |
| Tteok (rice cakes, box) | ₩5,000+ | Great for takeaway/gifts |
| Draft beer (maekju) | ₩3,000–₩4,000 | Available at most food stalls |

A solid food tour — two pancakes, a yukhoe, mayak gimbap, and a beer — will run you roughly **₩25,000–₩35,000 per person** (approximately USD $18–$26 or EUR €16–€24, as of August 2026). That's a full Seoul food experience for the price of a fast-food meal back home.

> **Prices and menus change seasonally. Always confirm with the vendor on the day of your visit.**

## How to Navigate Gwangjang Market Like a Local (Step-by-Step)

The market can feel overwhelming on your first visit — here's how I recommend tackling it.

1. **Arrive early.** The food alley gets seriously packed by noon on weekends. Aim to arrive by **10:00–10:30 AM** for the best seats and freshest ingredients.
2. **Enter from the Jongno 5-ga subway exit.** Take Seoul Metro Line 1 to **Jongno 5-ga Station (종로5가역)**, Exit 8. The market entrance is a 2-minute walk.
3. **Head straight to the central food alley.** Once inside, follow the smell of frying oil — it'll lead you directly to the main covered corridor lined with food stalls on both sides.
4. **Pick a stall with a visible queue.** Locals know the best spots. If a stall has a line, join it. The most famous bindaetteok stalls are clustered near the center of the main alley.
5. **Sit down, don't just grab and go.** Most stalls have low stools and shared tables. Sit, order multiple dishes, and eat communally — this is how it's meant to be done.
6. **Bring cash.** While some stalls now accept card payments (as of August 2026), many vendors still prefer **cash (Korean Won)**. There are ATMs near the market entrances.
7. **Save room for the upper floor.** The second floor houses fabric and clothing stalls, but the surrounding perimeter has more snack vendors selling tteok, dried goods, and seasonal treats.

## Best Time to Visit and What to Know Before You Go

Summer is a sweaty but spectacular time to visit Gwangjang — here's what's happening right now.

As of **August 2026**, Gwangjang Market is fully operational and buzzing with summer energy. The market is open **daily from approximately 9:00 AM to 11:00 PM**, though individual stall hours vary. Some of the most beloved food stalls close early when they run out of ingredients — another reason to go in the morning.

A few practical notes:
- **It gets hot.** The main food alley is covered but not air-conditioned. A hand fan and light clothing are a must in August.
- **It's not all food.** Gwangjang is also famous for **vintage clothing and fabrics** — the second floor is a treasure trove for fashion enthusiasts hunting for vintage Korean textiles and silk hanbok fabric.
- **Language is not a barrier.** Most food stall vendors have laminated menus with photos and English labels. Pointing and holding up fingers works perfectly.
- **It became internationally famous** in part thanks to its feature on Netflix's *Street Food Asia* (2019), which spotlighted the market's legendary bindaetteok vendors. You can still find some of those featured stalls today.

For official visitor information including maps and accessibility details, check the [Korea Tourism Organization's official page](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/) and [Korea.net's cultural coverage](https://www.korea.net/).

## The One-Line Takeaway (And Your Pre-Visit Checklist)

**Gwangjang Market is the single best place in Seoul to experience Korean street food culture — affordable, authentic, and completely alive.**

Before you go, run through this checklist:

- [ ] Withdraw Korean Won cash (at least ₩40,000–₩50,000 per person)
- [ ] Take Line 1 to Jongno 5-ga Station, Exit 8
- [ ] Arrive before 11:00 AM on weekdays or 10:00 AM on weekends
- [ ] Prioritize: bindaetteok → mayak gimbap → yukhoe (in that order)
- [ ] Bring a small bag for tteok or fabric shopping on the upper floor
- [ ] Confirm opening hours and any seasonal closures via [VisitKorea](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/) before your trip

The market has been feeding Seoul for over 120 years and shows absolutely no sign of slowing down. Go hungry, go curious, and go soon — this is the kind of food experience you'll be telling people about for years.
