## The Squid Game Effect Is Still Going Strong in 2026

The ripple effect of *Squid Game* on Korean tourism shows no signs of stopping — and Season 2's 2024 release followed by Season 3 in 2025 only poured fuel on the fire.

According to the [Korea Tourism Organization (VisitKorea)](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr), inbound tourists citing K-content as a primary travel motivation hit a record high as of July 2026, with **Korean drama and film tourism accounting for an estimated 23% of all leisure travel to Korea** — up from around 14% in 2023. That's a seismic shift.

The *Squid Game* franchise specifically has turned a handful of otherwise ordinary Korean locations into global pilgrimage sites. When I visited the **Gongjicheon stream in Incheon** on a Tuesday morning in July 2026, there were already tour groups from Thailand, Germany, and the United States queuing up to photograph the staircase featured in the show. A Tuesday. In July heat. That tells you everything.

What's driving this? Three things: the explosive global reach of Netflix K-content, the Korean government's active investment in drama tourism infrastructure, and a new generation of fans who want *experiences*, not just souvenirs.

When I went in late July 2026, the costume rental lines were running about 45 minutes long on weekdays. Book ahead online.

For free spots, the **Gongjicheon staircase in Incheon** is the most Instagrammable and easiest to reach via subway (Gyulhyeon Station on Line 1). The walk from the station takes about 10 minutes. Go before 9 AM if you want the shot without 50 strangers in the frame.

## Beyond Squid Game: Other K-Drama Locations Blowing Up Right Now

*Squid Game* is the headline act, but Korean drama tourism in 2026 is a whole festival, not just one band.

**"When the Stars Gossip" (2025)** turned Nami Island into an even bigger destination than it already was — and that's saying something. The island's fairy-tale tree lanes are now dotted with cast photo boards, and the ferry ride over feels like a fan event on weekends.

**"Queen of Tears" filming spots** in Jeju Island have driven a measurable spike in Jeju tourism packages, with travel agencies reporting a **30–40% increase in Jeju drama tour bookings** compared to 2024 (as of June 2026, per multiple Korean travel agency reports).

And then there's **Bukchon Hanok Village in Seoul** — a perennial favorite that keeps appearing in new dramas every season. It's featured in so many shows at this point that it almost functions as its own cinematic universe. The Seoul Metropolitan Government has actually introduced a [timed-entry system for Bukchon](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr) (as of 2025, still in place in 2026) to manage overtourism, so check the booking portal before you show up.

**Other rising spots in 2026:**
- **Gamcheon Culture Village, Busan** — featured in multiple 2025 romance dramas
- **Ihwa Mural Village, Seoul** — indie drama favorite, great for evening visits
- **Suncheon Bay Wetland Reserve** — used in eco-themed K-dramas and stunning in its own right

## How to Plan Your Korean Drama Tourism Trip: A Step-by-Step Guide

Planning a K-drama filming location trip sounds overwhelming, but I've done it — here's how to keep it sane.

1. **Pick your dramas first.** Make a list of 2–3 shows and their key locations before you book anything. [Korea.net's dedicated Hallyu tourism section](https://www.korea.net/TalkaboutKorea/Korean-Life/view?articleId=1693) is a solid starting point for location lists.
2. **Cluster locations geographically.** Seoul spots (Dobong-gu, Bukchon, Ihwa) can be combined in a day. Yongin/Anyang are 30–60 minutes from Seoul by public transit — do those together. Jeju and Nami Island need dedicated days.
3. **Book paid attractions in advance.** Macha Studio, Daejanggeum Park, and Nami Island ferry tickets sell out on weekends. Use their official websites or Klook/Trazy for English-language booking.
4. **Time your visits.** Weekday mornings (before 10 AM) are dramatically less crowded at free outdoor spots. Paid studios are busy all day.
5. **Factor in travel time.** Korea's public transit is excellent, but drama locations are sometimes surprisingly spread out. Build buffer time.
6. **Download Naver Maps** (not just Google Maps — Naver is far more accurate for Korean addresses and transit routing).
7. **Budget realistically.** A two-day drama location day-trip from Seoul — including transport, one paid studio, meals, and a Bukchon walk — runs approximately **₩80,000–₩150,000 per person** depending on choices, as of August 2026.

## What the K-Drama Tourism Experience Is Actually Like (Honest Take)

Let me be real with you: some spots are magical, and some are a bit underwhelming without context.

The **Gongjicheon staircase** is genuinely striking — it has an eerie, cinematic quality even without the show's costume or camera angles. Standing there, you can absolutely feel why the production team chose it. **Macha Studio**, on the other hand, is best if you go all-in — rent the jumpsuit, do the photo shoot, commit to the bit. Visitors who wander through without engaging with the interactive elements often tell me it felt "smaller than expected."

The neighborhoods — Ssangmun-dong alley, Ihwa Village — are my personal favorites because they're still lived-in, real places. You're not in a theme park; you're in someone's community. Be respectful, keep noise down (especially near Bukchon's residential lanes), and buy something from local vendors. The economic benefit of drama tourism is real for these communities when tourists engage thoughtfully.

One thing that genuinely surprised me: **the community of fans you meet at these spots**. I've had impromptu conversations with travelers from Brazil, Indonesia, France, and the US, all drawn by the same shows. It's one of the more unexpectedly joyful aspects of K-drama tourism in 2026.
